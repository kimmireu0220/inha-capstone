Iterative portrait editing requires state updates that preserve or remove selected attributes without changing unrelated requirements. We implement bounded preservation and removal guards after a shared two-call extraction and review pipeline. The guards record evidence spans and abstain on unsupported expressions. On a frozen set S of eight synthetic dialogues comprising 32 turns, joint state accuracy was 27/32 for the common baseline, 27/32 for preservation alone, 28/32 for removal alone, and 29/32 for their combination. Correct final dialogue states increased from 6/8 to 7/8, with all gains occurring in two turns of one dialogue. On another frozen set T of eight dialogues, the combination improved joint accuracy from 28/32 to 29/32 and final dialogue accuracy from 7/8 to 8/8. Per-attribute error isolation added no further gain. An image study with two portrait references produced 66 unique outputs for 128 conditions. Primary 3B evaluation scores increased from 350/384 to 358/384, but six additional successes came from previously unknown judgments. A post-hoc 7B review scored 372/384 and 374/384 without unknown judgments, yet contradictory explanations remained. These results support complementary prevention of state-update errors within a restricted English grammar; they do not establish general conversational accuracy or overall image-quality improvement.
