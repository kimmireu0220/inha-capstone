Iterative portrait editing requires prompts to reflect requested changes while preserving or removing specified attributes. We apply explicit preservation and removal rules after a language model extracts and reviews the requested changes. Preservation retains the previous value, whereas removal sets an attribute to absent. In the preservation and removal rule comparison using eight synthetic English dialogues with 32 turns, all six attributes were correct in 27/32 turns for the baseline, 27/32 for preservation alone, 28/32 for removal alone, and 29/32 for their combination. In a replication using eight new dialogues and 32 turns, correct turns increased from 28/32 to 29/32. A separate complex-request experiment comprised 12 dialogues and 48 turns involving expressions and poses. For the condition combining these requests with attribute changes, preservation, removal, and restoration, correct turns increased from 4/16 after model review to 16/16 after rule correction. In this experiment, removal rules corrected confusion between removing an object and restoring its original state. An image study using two portrait references revealed inconsistencies between evaluator explanations and scores. The rules reduced state-update errors without additional model calls within the tested English patterns and synthetic dialogues.
